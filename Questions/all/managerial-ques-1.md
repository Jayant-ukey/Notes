### 1. Project-Based Questions

These are asked almost every time.

* Explain your current project.
* What exactly is your role?
* What is the architecture of your application?
* What was the most challenging issue you solved?
* Tell me about a production issue you handled.
* What was your contribution compared to the team's contribution?
* How many team members are there?
* Have you interacted with clients directly?
* If I call your manager, what would they say about you?

Candidate reports repeatedly mention that Infosys interviewers spend significant time discussing real project experience. ([Glassdoor][1])

---

### 2. Team and Conflict Management

Common managerial questions:

* Describe a conflict with a teammate and how you resolved it.
* What would you do if a team member was not performing?
* How do you help junior developers?
* Have you mentored anyone?
* What if your teammate disagrees with your solution?

These questions are aimed at evaluating collaboration and leadership potential. ([Reddit][2])

---

### 3. Deadline and Pressure Situations

Very common in Infosys managerial rounds:

* What will you do if the project deadline is unrealistic?
* How do you handle pressure?
* Have you ever worked late to meet a release deadline?
* What if two critical tasks are assigned simultaneously?
* What if production fails just before deployment?

One candidate specifically reported questions around handling deadlines and pressure situations. ([Reddit][3])

---

### 4. Client-Facing Scenarios

Because Infosys is service-based, these are important.

* Have you spoken directly with clients?
* How would you handle changing requirements from a client?
* What would you do if the client rejected your solution?
* How do you explain technical issues to non-technical stakeholders?

These types of questions are repeatedly mentioned in managerial-round discussions. ([Reddit][2])

---

### 5. Career and Motivation Questions

Expect:

* Why do you want to leave your current company?
* Why Infosys?
* Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
* Are you comfortable relocating?
* Are you willing to learn new technologies?
* What if the project requires a different technology stack?

Candidates reported questions about willingness to learn and long-term goals. ([Reddit][3])

---

### 6. Resume-Based Technical Questions

Many candidates say the "managerial" round is sometimes a mix of managerial + technical.

For a Java developer with 4 years' experience, managers may ask:

* Why did you choose Spring Boot?
* Explain a microservice you developed.
* How did you improve performance?
* What Java 8 features do you use daily?
* How do you handle exception management?
* Explain a difficult bug you fixed.

Several candidates reported Java 8, Spring Boot, project architecture, and project-based technical discussions even during managerial rounds. ([Glassdoor][1])
